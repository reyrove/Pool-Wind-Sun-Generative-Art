00 / Statement Artist Statement

A still pool remembers every drop that fell into it.

I am an Iranian-born artist working at the intersection of classical textile logic and generative systems. My work begins with a simple observation: the woven surface — repetitive, mathematically structured, infinitely variable — has always been a form of computation, long before computers.

Pool Wind Sun simulates a wave equation — the classical model of ripples on a still surface. One or several oscillating sources strike a rectangular pool; the resulting waves reflect off the boundaries and interfere with one another, producing a complex field of constructive and destructive nodes.

Where the classical pool is a single still moment, this system freezes it — running the simulation forward for thirty to seventy time-steps, then capturing the field. A gradient palette sweeps the grid diagonally, from a deep blue at the upper-left to a bright cyan at the lower-right, so the wave field reads as both fluid and luminous. Each piece is defined by a numeric seed and can be regenerated, documented, and adapted for textile, print, and surface applications.

On AI tools — where I encountered technical obstacles, I turned to AI tools for debugging and code optimization. Every structural, aesthetic, and conceptual decision remained my own.

01 / Seed

Variation

A numeric seed initializes the grid dimensions (350–400 columns), the wave speed, the time-step, the number of oscillating sources, and the background tone. Each output is regenerated and documented as a distinct, traceable work.

02 / Interference

Geometry

A 2D wave equation is stepped forward 30–70 times. Sources inject oscillating values into the grid; the waves spread and reflect off the boundary, interfering with one another into a field of nodes and antinodes.

03 / Surface

Application

The resulting composition becomes a surface-design asset — explored across garments, textile prints, wallpaper and other pattern-driven applications.
